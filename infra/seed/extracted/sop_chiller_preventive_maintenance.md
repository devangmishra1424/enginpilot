## 1. Scope

This SOP covers non-invasive and controlled preventive-maintenance inspection activities for the curated Carrier 30XA chiller asset set.

This SOP does not authorize refrigerant charging, recovery, leak repair, electrical-panel access without approved lockout/tagout, compressor disassembly, coil cleaning using an unverified pressure or chemical, controls changes, safety changes, setpoint changes, lubrication, glycol adjustment, or refrigerant handling unless confirmed for the installed model and performed by qualified personnel.

## 2. Personnel and Safety Requirements

1.  Use qualified personnel trained for chiller equipment and site safety procedures.
2.  Follow site PPE requirements.
3.  Apply lockout/tagout before electrical inspection, electrical-panel access, or invasive maintenance.
4.  Follow refrigerant-handling regulations and site procedures.
5.  Do not bypass safety devices, pressure switches, alarms, or control interlocks.
6.  Escalate abnormal refrigerant, compressor, electrical, vibration, or control conditions to qualified service personnel.

## 3. Pre-Inspection Review

1.  Confirm the chiller asset ID, model, location, and inspection date.
2.  Review current alarms, fault history, operating history, maintenance records, and open work orders.
3.  Record operating status, available temperature readings, pressure indications, power readings, and alarm conditions where available.
4.  Confirm whether the unit is operating, in standby, or isolated before proceeding.
5.  Verify that annual or seasonal maintenance activity is authorized by the site maintenance plan.

## 4. Condenser Coil Inspection

1.  Inspect condenser coils for dirt, debris, bent fins, visible damage, restricted airflow, or obstruction.
2.  Inspect the surrounding area for conditions that may reduce airflow or introduce debris.
3.  Record the condition of the coils and airflow path.
4.  Schedule coil cleaning by qualified personnel when inspection indicates fouling or restricted airflow.

[NEEDS OEM CONFIRMATION: Carrier-approved cleaning method, pressure, chemical, water quality, fin-cleaning method, and cleaning frequency.]

## 5. Compressor and Refrigerant-Related Inspection

1.  Review compressor-related alarms, trips, operating history, and abnormal-event records.
2.  Listen for abnormal compressor noise, unusual vibration, cycling behavior, or operating instability.
3.  Inspect visible refrigerant circuit components only where safely accessible and without disturbing the system.
4.  Record visible signs of oil leakage, refrigerant-related alarms, damaged insulation, or abnormal pipe condition.
5.  Escalate suspected refrigerant leaks, compressor faults, oil-related concerns, or abnormal vibration to qualified service personnel.
6.  Do not perform refrigerant charging, recovery, leak repair, or compressor service under this SOP.

## 6. Electrical and Controls Inspection

1.  Review available control-panel alarms, warnings, fault codes, and operational status.
2.  Verify that alarms and displayed values are recorded before any reset is considered.
3.  Do not reset recurring alarms without documenting the condition and confirming the cause through approved procedures.
4.  Perform electrical-panel inspection only under approved lockout/tagout and qualified-personnel requirements.
5.  Do not alter controls, safeties, setpoints, or control logic under this SOP.

## 7. Power and Cooling-Output Review

1.  Record available power-input readings and cooling-output readings where monitoring equipment is available.
2.  Compare current readings against prior readings or approved site baselines where available.
3.  Investigate significant unexplained increases in power consumption, reduced cooling performance, repeated alarms, or unstable operation.
4.  Record observations and escalate unexplained degradation.

## [NEEDS OEM CONFIRMATION: COP calculation method, efficiency threshold, and degradation method.]

Do not record, report, or use an "80% COP threshold" as an OEM requirement.

## 8. Seasonal and Annual Maintenance

1.  Perform seasonal maintenance only according to the installed-unit documentation and approved site maintenance plan.
2.  Confirm refrigerant type, glycol requirements, water-side requirements, electrical frequency, voltage, lubrication requirements, and control configuration before performing seasonal work.
3.  Do not assume that values from another Carrier 30XA variant apply to the installed unit.
4.  Record all annual and seasonal maintenance actions in the site maintenance system.

Controlled Note: The curated Carrier document set may include configuration or regional differences. Electrical frequency, voltage, refrigerant, glycol, differential-pressure, lubrication, and coil-cleaning requirements must be verified against the installed-unit documentation.

## 9. Escalation Conditions

Escalate for active faults, repeated alarms, suspected refrigerant or oil leaks, abnormal compressor noise or vibration, restricted condenser airflow, electrical damage, unexplained power increase, reduced cooling performance, or any work requiring refrigerant handling, internal service, electrical-panel access, or controls modification.

## 10. Documentation and Post-Inspection Verification

1.  Record asset ID, date, time, inspector, operating status, alarms, readings, and observations.
2.  Record coil condition, compressor observations, visible refrigerant-related concerns, and control-panel status.
3.  Record all escalations, work orders, and recommended follow-up actions.
4.  Confirm that the unit remains in a safe operating condition after inspection.
5.  Do not mark the inspection complete if a fault, alarm, safety concern, or unresolved degradation condition remains open.
